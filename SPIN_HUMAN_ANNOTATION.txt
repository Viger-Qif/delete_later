NEGOTIATION LAB — SPIN HUMAN ANNOTATION PACK
Version: 1.0
Purpose: calibrate the SPIN learning checker and LLM-RAG coach on real human answers.
Active methodology: SPIN only.

============================================================
1. HOW TO USE THIS FILE
============================================================

One BLOCK = one human answer to one exercise.

Before annotating:
1. Read the context and instruction.
2. Read the learner answer once without comparing it to the example.
3. Decide what the learner was trying to do.
4. Mark the observable criteria.
5. Choose one overall label.
6. Write short feedback that would help the learner try again.

Do not grade grammar, spelling, personal style, or exact similarity to the example.
Accept natural paraphrases when they perform the same negotiation move.
Do not reward a beautiful sentence that does not answer the task.
Do not punish a short sentence if it performs the correct move.

Use only the information in the exercise context. Do not infer facts about the learner.
Remove names, company names, emails, salaries, phone numbers, and other personal data.

============================================================
2. OVERALL LABELS
============================================================

strong
  The answer performs the requested move and is usable without a major change.
  Minor wording improvements are allowed.

usable
  The answer is directionally correct and could work, but one important element
  is weak, vague, premature, too broad, or poorly connected to the context.
  The learner should normally be allowed to continue after one focused hint.

needs_focus
  The answer does not yet perform the requested move, jumps to a solution,
  misses the target SPIN stage, or is too vague to use.

off_task
  The answer is unrelated to the negotiation task or answers a different question.

unsafe_or_boundary
  The answer contains an ultimatum, threat, harassment, fabricated offer,
  personal attack, or another boundary violation. Do not praise it as a strong move.

If you are between two labels, choose the more generous label and lower confidence.
The goal is to improve learning, not to win an argument with the learner.

============================================================
3. CRITERION VALUES
============================================================

pass
  Clearly present in the answer.

partial
  Present, but weak, vague, too broad, or not fully connected to context.

fail
  Missing or contradicted by the answer.

not_applicable
  The criterion does not belong to this exercise. Use only when the exercise
  explicitly makes the criterion optional.

Common SPIN criteria:
- open_question: asks an open question or a natural multi-part question;
- situation: explores how the current process/context works;
- problem: explores a concrete difficulty, friction, delay, risk, or limitation;
- listening: reflects or accurately checks what the other person just said;
- implication: connects a recognized problem to a consequence for work, time,
  team, risk, cost, responsibility, or result;
- need_payoff: lets the other person formulate the benefit of change;
- linked_to_context: question is connected to the specific fact in the prompt;
- specific: asks for a concrete example, stage, number, date, criterion, or result;
- no_pitch: does not prematurely advertise a product or jump to a solution;
- calibrated: avoids threats, absolutes, drama, blame, and exaggeration;
- sequence: follows the requested SPIN order.

============================================================
4. REQUIRED FIELDS FOR EACH ANNOTATION BLOCK
============================================================

case_id: unique id, for example spin-human-0001
exercise_id: exact id from learning.json
unit_id: exact unit id from learning.json
annotator_id: anonymous code, for example annotator-a
context: copy from the exercise or use the supplied scenario context
instruction: copy the learner-facing instruction
learner_answer: exact answer, with personal data removed
intent: what the learner appears to be trying to do
overall_label: strong | usable | needs_focus | off_task | unsafe_or_boundary
criteria:
  open_question: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  situation: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  problem: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  listening: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  implication: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  need_payoff: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  linked_to_context: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  specific: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  no_pitch: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  calibrated: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  sequence: pass | partial | fail | not_applicable
  # Keep only the criteria that are present in the exercise rubric.
feedback: one or two sentences addressed to the learner
next_hint: one concrete thing to try on the next attempt
acceptable_alternative: yes | no
acceptable_alternative_reason: why this answer is or is not a valid paraphrase
ideal_reply: one possible improved reply; do not imply it is the only correct wording
annotator_confidence: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5
needs_adjudication: yes | no
adjudication_note: leave empty unless annotators disagree

============================================================
7. RECOMMENDED CALIBRATION PROCESS
============================================================

Pilot batch:
- 5 answers per unit;
- 6 units;
- 30 answers total;
- two annotators label the same pilot batch;
- discuss every disagreement before scaling.

Full calibration batch:
- 20–30 answers per unit;
- 120–180 answers total;
- at least 20% double-labelled by a second person;
- store disagreements instead of silently deleting them.

After annotation, calculate:
- agreement on overall_label;
- agreement on each core criterion;
- false-negative rate for natural paraphrases;
- percentage of `usable` answers that become `strong` after one hint;
- whether the next recommended exercise matches the human-labelled weak skill.

Do not optimize only for agreement with the current expert checker. If humans agree
that the checker is too strict, update the checker and keep the human labels as the
calibration reference.

============================================================
8. HOW HARD IS MANUAL LABELING?
============================================================

A first pilot is easy if the annotator understands basic SPIN:
- 10–15 minutes to read this guide and calibrate on the examples;
- about 3–5 minutes per answer during the first batch;
- 30-answer pilot: roughly 2–3 hours for one annotator;
- double-labelled pilot: roughly 4–6 total person-hours.

A full 120–180-answer calibration is moderate, not research-lab-hard:
- roughly 6–15 hours for one experienced annotator;
- roughly 12–30 person-hours when 20% or more is double-labelled;
- most time is spent discussing ambiguous answers, not filling fields.

A negotiator, sales trainer, manager, or researcher can do the first pass.
A senior negotiation expert should review the disagreements and the examples used
as gold cases. We do not need a PhD-level annotation team for the first SPIN pilot.
